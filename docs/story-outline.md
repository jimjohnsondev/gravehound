## Premise

A boy turns 18 in a forest village, a goblin steals his dog that same night, and he learns the dog has been carrying his death since he was a baby.

**Logline:** To rescue his dog from a dying necromancer, a young man must cross a haunted forest and descend into the Underreach, only to learn that bringing the dog home means facing the death it has kept from him for eighteen years.

**Tone:** a dark fairytale. It is quiet, candlelit and folkloric, more Grimm than heroic fantasy. The stakes are personal: one dog, one family, one tower. The scenery is dark and the characters glow against it, which fits the art direction you already chose.

**Title:** *Gravehound*. The Underreach is the land of the dead below the forest.

## The secret

Ash, the dog, is a gravehound: a guide of the dead who has kept the hero alive since infancy. That is why the necromancer wants him.

**What happened 18 years ago.** A fever took the village one winter. The hero's parents died of it, and so did their baby. Ash was the gravehound sent to walk the three of them down into the Underreach. He took the parents, but he would not take the child. Grandmother found the baby breathing in the snow beside a black dog no one in Hollin owned. That night she went to the standing stone in the forest and made a bargain. The hound would carry the child's death inside himself until the boy came of age. After that, the debt would be called.

**Ash was there before.** A gravehound walks with a household long before it is needed, half-seen, the way a shadow is. That is why Ash appears in the parents' wedding portrait, painted years before the fever. Grandmother only understood it after the bargain, and she says so in her letter.

**Why the goblin comes on his 18th birthday.** The bargain ends at midnight. A death that has been carried is loose for the first time in 18 years, and anyone holding the hound can claim it.

**Why the necromancer wants it.** Master Halloway, the Bone-Reeve, has been dying for 60 years and keeps himself going with stolen scraps of life. A cheated death is the strongest thing in his craft. If he owns the hero's death, he can spend it in place of his own and never go down into the dark. A gravehound is also the only key to the Underreach door, which Halloway wants for a second reason (see Cast).

**Clues planted from the first screen:**

- Ash is shown in the parents' wedding portrait on the cottage wall, painted before the fever. Nobody remarks on it. (Deliberate: see "Ash was there before".)
- Villagers mention how old the dog must be by now, and Grandmother changes the subject.
- Ash will not cross the churchyard. Animals go quiet when he passes.
- Every time the hero dies, a black dog leads him out of the netherworld.
- Grandmother's dialog on birthday night is warm, but she keeps glancing at the door and won't let Ash outside.

## Cast

Seven characters carry the story. Every one of them has a stake in the hound.

| Character | Role | What they want | Secret or turn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The hero (player-named) | 18, lives with Grandmother in Hollin | Get Ash back | He should have died as a baby; he's living on borrowed time |
| Ash | The dog; secretly a gravehound | To keep the boy alive | Releasing him means the boy's death comes due |
| Grandmother Wren | Raised the hero | For the boy to live a full life | She made the bargain and has dreaded this birthday for 18 years |
| Maren | Barmaid at the Lantern & Thorn inn | Her little brother back | Her brother Nib became the goblin; she has been inside the tower once and escaped |
| Nib | The goblin who steals Ash | To be human again | A village boy Halloway cursed during the fever; he remembers playing with Ash |
| Master Halloway, the Bone-Reeve | Necromancer in the tower | To escape his own death | He was Hollin's healer during the fever and was driven out when he failed. He plans to open the Underreach and raise the fever-dead as servants, the hero's parents among them |
| Tobin Marsh | Village hunter (the rival) | Halloway's reward, and Maren | Paid to hunt down Ash if the dog escapes; he can be won over or must be beaten |

The rival works against you without being a monster. Tobin reaches places first, shuts doors behind him, lies to villagers about you, and keeps showing up at the inn to court Maren.

## Map

The world is eight hand-illustrated locations in a line, from home to the tower, with the Underreach beneath them. Each location is one or more maps: single screens for still, intimate scenes, and scrolling maps for places to explore (see the Gameplay & Controls Spec). The order is fixed, but a seed can shuffle side details: which villager needs what, where items are hidden, and the weather.

## Beat sheet

The story runs in three acts. Each one ends with a revelation that changes what "rescue the dog" means.

**Act I: The Theft** (cottage, village, inn)

1. Before supper, Grandmother sends the hero to the wood's edge for kindling, with Ash at his heels. A wisp drifts between the trees. He has no weapon yet, so he learns to sneak past it; dying here is his first trip to the Underreach. Back home it's his birthday supper. Grandmother gives him his father's knife and is oddly tense. She bars the door.
2. At midnight Ash howls and the window bursts in. A small goblin drags him into the forest.
3. In the village, folk say goblins come from the tower past the Old Wood. Tobin laughs at the hero, then leaves for the woods himself.
4. At the inn, Maren listens. She knows the way and won't say why. She gives him a lantern, and later she will join him.
5. **Turn:** Grandmother begs him not to go, then tells him only half the truth. "That dog has kept you safe. Bring him home, whatever anyone tells you."

**Act II: The Trail** (Old Wood, chapel, warrens)

6. In the Old Wood, the standing stone shows carved marks of a bargain and a date: the night of the fever, the winter he was a baby.
7. At the Drowned Chapel, Halloway's healing ledger lists the fever-dead. The hero's parents are there, and so is a third line on the same date: *their son, infant.*
8. **Midpoint revelation:** the hero realizes he died with them, and that Ash is why he's alive.
9. Maren admits the goblin is her brother Nib, taken during the fever.
10. In the Warrens, Nib has been feeding Ash and crying over him. Halloway made him steal the dog, and Nib could not refuse. You choose whether to spare Nib or fight him. Sparing him unlocks help later.
11. Tobin ambushes you at the warrens' exit and tries to take Ash's collar as proof for his bounty. You can fight him, bribe him, or shame him into helping with Maren's word.

**Act III: The Tower and Below** (tower, Underreach)

12. You climb Halloway's tower. Each floor shows what he has become, ending with a room of the fever-dead's belongings, kept like trophies.
13. Halloway is gaunt and nearly dead. He isn't proud. He's afraid. He offers the hero a deal: "Give me the death. You'll live, and I'll give your parents back to you as servants."
14. Halloway forces the Underreach door open using Ash. Everyone falls through.
15. The finale is in the Underreach. The hero meets his parents' shades. Halloway's spell is pulling the dead upward, and the hound is chained in the middle.
16. The ending choice comes next (see Endings).

## Romance

The romance with Maren is optional. It's built from small choices in dialog, and in the finale it can change the outcome.

The two of them are equals on parallel quests: he's after a dog, she's after a brother. The romance grows from what you choose to do for each other, not from flattery.

- **Four intimate moments.** Late-night talk at the inn (Act I). Sharing the lantern in the Old Wood (Act II). Telling her what the ledger said (midpoint). Sparing Nib for her sake (Act II).
- **Affection is a hidden counter.** It goes up when you're honest with her, protect Nib, and stand up to Tobin. Lying to her or taking Tobin's bribe lowers it.
- **The confession.** At the base of the tower, if affection is high, she says she's coming down into the dark with him. If it's low, she stays to hold the door.
- **Tobin is the contrast.** He offers Maren an easy, safe life in the village. If you keep winning her trust, he resents it, and that can push him toward Halloway.

## Endings

There are four endings, decided by one final choice in the Underreach plus whether you spared Nib and won Maren. The best one feels earned. None of them pretends death can be undone for free.

| Ending | How you get it | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Hound's Due** (true ending) | Free Ash, with Nib spared and Maren beside you | Nib breaks the chain and Ash goes for Halloway instead. The necromancer's 60 stolen years pay off the boy's debt. The parents' shades say goodbye and walk down with Ash. Nib turns back into a boy. The hero and Maren climb back up into the dawn. |
| **Borrowed No Longer** | Free Ash without the allies | The debt comes due. The hero walks down into the dark with his parents and his dog. Maren, or Grandmother, carries the lantern back home. Bittersweet and peaceful. |
| **The Bone-Reeve's Bargain** | Accept Halloway's deal | You live and your parents return as hollow servants. Halloway lives forever. In the epilogue you are the tower's steward. The darkest ending. |
| **Mine to Keep** | Grab Ash and run, refusing everyone | You make it home alive with your dog. The debt still has to be paid, though, and Grandmother is found cold in her chair the next morning. |

## Story and mechanics

The netherworld-on-death rule you already designed becomes the story's central metaphor, and getting harder over the course of the game is the plot.

- **Death before the theft** (tutorial, on the kindling errand). You fall into the Underreach and a black dog leads you straight out. It's a quick and eerie hint at the secret.
- **Death after the theft.** No guide comes. You have to find your own way up through a short maze of shades. Each time you return, you've lost a little: some gold, or a temporary stat drain, never permanent progress.
- **The shades remember you.** After the midpoint, two pale figures appear near the exit path when you die. They're your parents. With more deaths they say more, so dying becomes a way to tell the story.
- **Leveling.** Keep it abbreviated: stats are Might, Wits and Heart. Heart affects dialog and Maren's affection checks.
- **Combat stays light.** One button, unlocked when Grandmother gives the hero his father's knife. Fight goblins, wisps, and Halloway's bone-hounds. Most encounters can be avoided by talking or sneaking.
- **Food** is a temporary buff. Maren's inn stew, Grandmother's honey cakes and forest mushrooms (a gamble) all work.
- **The rival on the map.** Tobin shows up one map ahead of you at key points. He may have looted a chest, set a snare, or turned a villager against you. Beating him to a place earns a small reward.

**Side quests** (one or two per location, each feeding the main story):

- **The miller's widow** asks you to lay her husband's shade to rest at the chapel. This teaches the Underreach rules.
- **The churchyard sexton** has been seeing empty graves. Following the trail leads to Halloway's grave-robbing.
- **Nib's toy.** Find the wooden horse Maren carved for him. Giving it to him is the key to sparing him.
- **Grandmother's letter** is hidden in the cottage and only appears after the midpoint. It tells the full truth about the bargain in her own words, including that Ash had walked with the family since before the hero's parents married.
- **Tobin's debt.** Learn why he needs Halloway's gold (a sick mother). Paying the debt or curing her turns him into an ally for the finale.
